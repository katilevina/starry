# Cover Letter Examples

This file contains examples of bad and good cover letters with analysis of why they work or don't work.

---

# ❌ Example 1: Bad Cover Letter (Networking Spam)

```
Кира, привет! Я в активном поиске работы и хотела уточнить, возможно у тебя есть на примете какие то хорошие вакансии?

Мне пока предлагают в основном сейлз всяких инфлюенсеров и продакшена, но мне не очень это интересно

Заранее спасибо за помощь!
```

## Why This is Bad:

* This isn't really a cover letter
* Person is writing for the first time (we're not acquainted)
* Doesn't state who they are professionally
* Doesn't say what they're looking for
* Only mentions what they DON'T want
* No CV attached
* No specific achievements or skills mentioned

## ✅ What They Should Have Done:

Write a concise cover letter about who they are as a professional + what they're looking for + attach CV immediately.

# ❌ Example 2: Bad Cover Letter (Generic Template)

```
Dear [name]:

I'm writing in response to your recently advertised position for a [role title]. I am very interested in this opportunity with [company] and believe that my qualifications, education and professional experience would make me a strong candidate for the position.

I am a [insert personal characteristics] professional who [add more information on how you can add value to future company]. (You can insert short version of your career profile here).

Enclosed is my resume that more fully details my background and work experience, and how they relate to your position. As you can see, [pick out a few key details or experiences that align with the specific requirements of the advertised role].

I firmly believe that I can be a valuable asset to your team. I welcome the opportunity to speak with you about this position and how my experience could help [insert name of company] achieve its goals.

Thank you in advance for your consideration.

Kind regards,

[Your name]
```

## Why This is Bad:

* **Too much fluff** — every sentence is generic
* **No specifics** — could be sent to any company
* **No personality** — sounds robotic
* **Template placeholders** — "[insert personal characteristics]" not filled out
* **Focus on self** ("I believe," "I welcome") instead of company needs
* **Empty phrases** — "valuable asset," "achieve its goals"
* **No metrics** — vague statements about experience
* **No company research** — nothing specific about the company

# ✅ Example 3: Good Cover Letter (Concise & Specific)

```
Hi Michelle,

I'm a huge fan of what you guys are doing at Zenefits! I know how hard finding the right person can be so I'll keep this short and sweet. Here are the top 4 reasons I think you should consider me for the Growth Content Marketing Manager role:

I've written for B2B companies, such as LinkedIn, to generate leads through owned and earned content

I've managed a team of 5 writers and have a B.A. in Creative Writing

I've worked with startups from the ground up to over 1 million subscribers

I'm an avid student of Tim Ferriss, Ramit Sethi, Derek Sivers, and Seth Godin and I'm always upgrading my skills regarding SEO, LTK optimization, and other best practices

I'm excited for the opportunity to work with you and I'd love to hear more about the position and see if there's a good fit.

Thank you,
Bogdan Zlatkov
```

## Why This is Good:

* **Brief, no fluff** — respects reader's time
* **Human language** — "huge fan," "short and sweet"
* **Obvious research** — knows about Zenefits
* **Specific achievements** — team size, subscriber count
* **Bullets complement CV** — doesn't repeat it
* **Relevant to role** — Content Marketing Manager requirements addressed
* **Shows personality** — mentions learning sources
* **Clear metrics** — "5 writers," "1 million subscribers"
* **Personalized** — addresses Michelle by name
* **Call to action** — wants to discuss fit

# ✅ Example 4: Good Cover Letter (Motivation-Focused)

```
Hi Alex,

I'm writing to apply for the Senior Product Manager role at Duolingo. As someone who learned Spanish to fluency using your app (567-day streak!), I've experienced firsthand how product decisions impact learning outcomes.

Why I'm a strong fit:

Led redesign of learning platform that increased user engagement by 2.6× and improved completion rates from 12% to 34% for 50K+ active users

Managed roadmap prioritization for B2B SaaS product, coordinating cross-functional teams of 15+ engineers, designers, and researchers

Launched 3 major product features from concept to market, one of which won "Best UX" at EdTech Awards 2023

I'm particularly excited about Duolingo's mission to make education free and accessible. My experience in both B2C and B2B EdTech has taught me that the best products balance user delight with measurable learning outcomes — something Duolingo does exceptionally well.

I'd love to discuss how my background aligns with your team's goals.

Best,
Sarah Chen
sarah.chen@email.com
linkedin.com/in/sarahchen
```

## Why This is Good:

* **Personal connection** — actual user with specific streak count
* **Relevant achievements** — EdTech experience, platform redesign
* **Specific metrics** — 2.6× engagement, 12%→34% completion, 50K users
* **Company knowledge** — references Duolingo's mission
* **Industry insight** — shows understanding of EdTech space
* **Relevant awards** — "Best UX" adds credibility
* **Cross-functional experience** — team sizes mentioned
* **Clear contact info** — email + LinkedIn
* **Professional but warm** — balances competence with enthusiasm

# 📊 Comparison: Before & After

### Before (Generic):
```
I'm applying for the Product Manager role. I have 5 years of experience in product management and have worked on various projects. I'm a hard worker and would love to join your team.
```

### After (Specific):

```
Relevant experience:
• Led recommendation engine project that increased user retention by 43% for 2M+ monthly active users
• Prioritized product roadmap based on A/B tests, user research, and business metrics
• Collaborated with data science teams to launch ML-powered features
```

# 🎯 Key Takeaways

**Good cover letters:**
- Are specific to the company and role
- Include concrete metrics and achievements
- Show you've done research on the company
- Are concise (150-250 words)
- Use human language, not templates
- Complement the CV, don't repeat it

**Bad cover letters:**
- Are generic and could apply to any company
- Lack specific achievements or metrics
- Show no knowledge of the company
- Are full of fluff and empty phrases
- Sound robotic or templated
- Duplicate what's already in the CV

---

**💡 Remember:** Your cover letter is your first test assignment. Treat it as an opportunity to demonstrate that you can communicate effectively, pay attention to details, and genuinely care about this specific opportunity.
